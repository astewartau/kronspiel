The Rules, Illustrated

Kronspiel's movement is deliberately familiar; its four departures are not. Each one below is shown as a legal sequence replayed through the game's own engine. For the full text, see the rulebook; to play, the table is set.

I.The Aschenstuhl — the Empty Throne

“The only chair on the board that cannot be taken from you is the one no one is permitted to sit in.” — §2

The centre square belongs to no one, ever. Sliders cannot see past it; the Gesandter may leap it but never land on it; for the Krone it is a wall, never a blade. And a Bürger whose file runs into it — at f5 or f7 — may step diagonally around it: the game's one non-capturing diagonal. The figure shows both sides using the throne's flanks, ending with a capture on its very doorstep.

II.The Bürger's Ways

“One square forward; on its first move only, it may instead advance one, two, or three squares in a straight line.” — §4

The commoner's whole career in one staged sparring game: the first-move dash, the long march, the diagonal capture, and the reward at the end of the road — promotion, only ever to a Gesandter. There is no en passant in Kronspiel; the enemy here is polite, but nothing shown is outside the rules.

III.Die Flucht — the King's One Road

“An unmoved Krone may, once per game, leap up to three squares in a straight orthogonal line — provided every square he passes through and lands on is empty and unthreatened.” — §5

Once, and only once — and any ordinary Krone move forfeits it forever. In the study's simulations half of all games spent the Flucht; good players treat the unspent option as a piece in hand, and the Fool's Gate (below) shows what happens to a Krone whose own court blocks the road.

IV.Isolation — the Only Way to Win

“A Krone is never captured. He is only isolated.” — §1

No check, no checkmate, no capture of kings. At the start of a player's turn, their Krone is lost if every neighbouring square is shut — by either court's pieces, by the board's edge, by the throne — and the enemy holds at least one genuine claim: a blade on the Krone's own square, or a threat against a genuinely empty door. The figure below is the famous two-move miniature, with the engine's own door-count painted on the board: watch the green doors disappear.

For the full anatomy of a slow Isolation — nine moves, not a single capture — see the bloodless miniature in the Rules Commission; for why the correct dash survives this same attack, see the Far Door in the Opening Book.
