    The Rules, Briefly

    The board

    Eleven by eleven, ash-grey and bone-pale. The centre square — the Aschenstuhl, the Ash Seat — is permanently empty. No piece may ever occupy it, sliding pieces cannot see past it, and a Gesandter may leap over it but never land on it. For the Krone it behaves exactly as the edge of the board: a square he cannot step onto, but never an enemy hand at his throat. A Bürger whose file runs into the throne — standing at f5 or f7 — may step diagonally around it onto e6 or g6, capture or no capture: the game's one non-capturing diagonal step.

    The pieces

    • Krone — one square in any direction, onto open ground only. Never captured, and never captures; see Isolation.
    • Kanzler (×2) — any distance in any direction. Each side has two, flanking the Krone from the first move.
    • Marschall — any distance orthogonally.
    • Prälat — any distance diagonally.
    • Gesandter — the broken L-step; leaps over anything in its path.
    • Bürger — one square forward; on its first move only, it may instead advance one, two, or three squares in a straight line. Captures one square diagonally forward, at any time. A Bürger reaching the far rank promotes — only ever to a Gesandter. There is no en passant.

    Die Flucht

    An unmoved Krone may, once per game, leap up to three squares in a straight orthogonal line — over any ground, even a threatened square, provided every square he passes through is empty and the square he lands on is empty and unthreatened. Any ordinary Krone move forfeits the option forever.

    Soft Isolation. Isolation is judged before the court acts, and there is normally no reaction to it — but Die Flucht is the one exception. A Krone who would be Isolated, and is spared only by his unused Flucht, is not lost: he stands in Soft Isolation, and on his turn he must take the leap — the Crown quitting a besieged capitol for open country. Once his Flucht is spent, the same siege is a true Isolation.

    Isolation — the only way to win

    There is no check, no checkmate, and no stalemate-as-draw. At the start of every turn, before any piece moves, the Krone of the player to move is examined. He is Isolated — and the game instantly lost — when both of the following hold:

    1. Every square adjacent to him (including any square still reachable by Die Flucht) is occupied by a piece of either colour, threatened by an enemy piece, or off the board.
    2. Either the Krone's own square is directly threatened by an enemy piece, or at least one adjacent square that is genuinely empty — held by no piece of either colour — is threatened by an enemy piece.

    An enemy threat against a square already filled by the Krone's own court counts for nothing — that door was never going to open for him regardless. Only two things end a Krone's reign: a blade genuinely at his own throat, or a genuinely empty door that the enemy, and the enemy alone, has nailed shut. A Krone is never undone by his own house alone — he is undone by his own house under siege, and it makes no difference that his own men built most of the wall.

    The Krone is judged as if already lifted from his square. Every threat that concerns his movement or survival is tested as though he were not standing where he stands: a Kanzler bearing directly upon him threatens the ground behind him just as surely as the ground before him. A king does not shelter a doorway with his own body.

    The Krone cannot be taken. No move may capture a Krone — it is simply not a legal move, exactly as landing on the Aschenstuhl is not. A Krone may stand indefinitely on a threatened square; only Isolation ends his part in the game.

    The Krone takes no one. The courtesy is returned: the Krone may never capture, and moves only onto open ground. Kronspiel permits no reaction — a Krone does not answer a wall by tearing a brick from it, and an enemy piece at his elbow is simply part of the wall.

    There is no rescue. Isolation is judged before the court is given leave to act. A piece that could have captured the offending enemy, or blocked its line, does not save him — the move that would have done it always comes one turn too late. The one safeguard the rules provide: you may never voluntarily make a move that isolates your own Krone, just as the Krone may never step into an enemy's line.

    The Palsied Court. A player with no legal move at all — Krone included — loses at once, provided the enemy has a hand in it by the test above: a blade at the Krone's throat, or a threat against a genuinely empty square beside him. Paralysed under siege is fallen. Without any enemy touch, it is instead the Frozen Court, and drawn.

    Draws

    • The Long Siege — the same position, same player to move, a third time; either player may claim.
    • The Long Winter — fifty moves by each side with no Bürger moved and no capture; either player may claim.
    • The Empty Court — lone Krone against lone Krone; drawn automatically.
    • Mutual Ruin — both Krone isolated at the start of the same round; drawn, and considered the most honest outcome the board can produce.
    • The Frozen Court — no legal move remains, yet no enemy hand touches the wall: the court has choked itself still, alone. Drawn automatically.
    • Parley — a draw agreed between the players at any point.

    Conventions of this table

    The Bone Court moves first. Ranks run 1–11 from the Bone home rank; files run a–k from the left of the Bone player.
